# Junior Analyst Data Pack — FY2025 AI Capex Intensity

## Overview and Definitions

This brief establishes a data collection and first-level analysis of FY2025 AI capital-expenditure intensity across six companies — Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and Apple. It is not an investment opinion. The scope covers seven required FY2025 metrics per company: revenue, operating income, operating margin, operating cash flow, capex, free cash flow (FCF), and capex intensity. FCF is defined as operating cash flow minus capex; capex intensity is defined as capex divided by operating cash flow [S6]. Fiscal-year ends differ across the group: December for Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta; June for Microsoft; late January for NVIDIA; and late September for Apple [S5][S6]. Only the five provided sources are used; no external figures are introduced.

## Capex Trends

- **Amazon** (FYE Dec): Capex rose from $40.1B in FY2020 to $131.8B in FY2025 — an increase of $91.7B, or ~3.3x (~229%) [S5][S4][S6].
- **Alphabet** (FYE Dec): Capex rose from $22.3B in FY2020 to $91.4B in FY2025 — an increase of $69.1B, or ~4.1x (~310%) [S5][S4][S6].
- **Meta** (FYE Dec): Capex rose from $15.2B in FY2020 to $69.7B in FY2025 — an increase of $54.5B, or ~4.6x (~359%) [S5][S6].
- **Microsoft** (FYE Jun): Capex rose from $15.4B in FY2020 to $64.6B in FY2025 — an increase of $49.2B, or ~4.2x (~319%) [S5][S6].
- **NVIDIA** (FYE Jan): Capex rose from $1.0B in FY2022 (earliest available year in corpus; FY2020–FY2021 capex not disclosed) to $3.2B in FY2025 — an increase of $2.2B, or ~3.2x (~220%) over three years [S5][S3][S6].
- **Apple** (FYE Sep): Capex rose from $7.3B in FY2020 to $12.7B in FY2025 — an increase of $5.4B, or ~1.7x (~74%), the most modest growth of the six [S5][S6].

## Cross-Company Comparison

FY2025 capex intensity (Capex/OCF) spans a wide range across the six companies, from 5% to 94% [S6][S7]. Ranked from highest to lowest intensity: **Amazon 94% > Meta 60% > Alphabet 56% > Microsoft 47% > Apple 11% > NVIDIA 5%** [S7]. This ranking inverts almost perfectly when viewed through FCF generation: Apple leads with $98.8B, followed by Alphabet ($73.3B), Microsoft ($71.6B), NVIDIA ($60.9B), Meta ($46.1B), and Amazon last at $7.7B [S6][S7].

The margin-versus-intensity trade-off reveals three distinct profiles. **NVIDIA** pairs the highest operating margin (62.4%) with the lowest capex intensity (5%), converting nearly all operating cash flow into FCF ($60.9B) [S6][S7]. **Apple** combines a solid 32.0% margin with 11% intensity, producing the largest absolute FCF of the group ($98.8B) [S6][S7]. **Microsoft** (45.6% margin, 47% intensity) and **Meta** (41.4% margin, 60% intensity) occupy the middle ground, though Meta's higher intensity compresses its FCF ($46.1B) relative to its strong profitability [S6][S7]. **Alphabet** generates robust FCF ($73.3B) on the largest OCF base ($164.7B) despite a 32.0% margin and 56% intensity [S6][S7]. **Amazon** is the clear outlier: an 11.2% operating margin paired with 94% capex intensity leaves only $7.7B of FCF, as capex ($131.8B) nearly consumes all operating cash flow ($139.5B) [S6][S7].

In summary, high margins do not uniformly translate to high FCF when capex intensity is elevated. The hyperscalers (Amazon, Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft) cluster in the high-intensity, moderate-to-high-margin quadrant, while NVIDIA and Apple achieve superior FCF conversion through structurally lower capital requirements [S6][S7].
