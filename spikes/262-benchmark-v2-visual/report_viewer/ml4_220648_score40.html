Junior Analyst Data Pack — FY2025 AI Capex Intensity

Overview and Definitionscontrat · Overview

This data pack provides a concise, factual reference for a senior analyst covering FY2025 AI capital expenditure intensity across six companies: Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and Apple. Its purpose is data collection and first-level analysis only — it does not constitute an investment opinion. FY2025 is defined as the latest complete fiscal year available in the frozen source corpus [S1]. Fiscal-year ends differ across the six companies: December for Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta; June for Microsoft; late January for NVIDIA; and late September for Apple [S6]. All figures must therefore be compared on a fiscal-year basis, not a calendar basis.

Capex Trendscontrat · Capex Trends

Amazon increased its capital expenditures from $40.1B in FY2020 to $131.8B in FY2025, an absolute increase of $91.7B (approximately 3.3x) [S7][S1]. The trend shows a sharp rise in FY2021, a plateau through FY2022, a dip in FY2023, then steep acceleration in FY2024–FY2025, with FY2025 capex reaching 94% of operating cash flow [S7].

Alphabet grew capex from $22.3B in FY2020 to $91.4B in FY2025, an increase of $69.1B (approximately 4.1x) [S7][S1]. The trajectory was gradual growth from FY2020 through FY2023, followed by a step-change in FY2024 and a near-doubling again in FY2025; capex as a share of operating cash flow rose from 34% to 56% over the period [S7].

Meta raised capex from $15.2B in FY2020 to $69.7B in FY2025, an increase of $54.5B (approximately 4.6x) [S7][S1]. The path included steady early growth, a jump in FY2022, a pullback in FY2023, then strong growth in FY2024–FY2025; capex/OCF rose from 39% to 60% [S7]. Note that the corpus figure excludes finance-lease principal payments [S7][S1].

Microsoft increased capex from $15.4B in FY2020 to $64.6B in FY2025, a rise of $49.2B (approximately 4.2x) [S7][S1]. The trend shows consistent year-over-year increases, with the largest absolute jumps in FY2024 (+$16.4B) and FY2025 (+$20.1B); capex/OCF climbed from 25% to 47% [S7].

NVIDIA has no capex data for FY2020–FY2021 in the corpus; the earliest available year is FY2022 at $1.0B, rising to $3.2B in FY2025, an increase of $2.2B (approximately 3.2x) [S7][S1]. Capex remained in low single-digit billions through FY2024 before a modest rise in FY2025, representing only 5% of operating cash flow [S7].

Apple increased capex from $7.3B in FY2020 to $12.7B in FY2025, a rise of $5.4B (approximately 1.7x) [S7][S1]. This is the flattest trajectory of the six companies: capex oscillated within a narrow $7–13B band across the entire period, dipping in FY2024 before reaching a period high in FY2025, with capex/OCF staying in the 8–11% range [S7].

Highest capex intensity: Amazon, at 94% of operating cash flow directed to capex, leaving the lowest FCF in the group at $7.7B [S5]. Lowest capex intensity: NVIDIA at 5%, followed by Apple at 11% [S5].

Range across the group: The capex/OCF ratio spans 89 percentage points, from 5% (NVIDIA) to 94% (Amazon) [S5]. FCF ranges from $7.7B (Amazon) to $98.8B (Apple) — a spread of $91.1B [S5].

Observable patterns:

  • The three December fiscal-year-end companies (Amazon, Alphabet, Meta) cluster at the high end of capex intensity (56–94%), with Amazon notably above the other two [S5].
  • The two non-December FYE companies with moderate intensity — Microsoft (47%) and Apple (11%) — sit in the middle and lower-middle of the range [S5].
  • NVIDIA is a clear outlier on the low end, with capex representing only 5% of OCF despite $64.1B in operating cash flow [S5].
  • FCF does not move in lockstep with capex intensity: Apple generates the highest FCF ($98.8B) at low intensity, while Amazon generates the lowest FCF ($7.7B) at the highest intensity; Alphabet and Microsoft report similar FCF ($73.3B and $71.6B) at markedly different intensity levels (56% vs. 47%) [S5].

Revenue scale differs across the group — from $130.5B (NVIDIA) to $716.9B (Amazon) [S2] — but the capex/OCF and FCF patterns above are reported as computed from FY2025 actuals without adjustment for revenue size.
