# FY2025 AI Capex Intensity — Junior Analyst Data Pack

## Overview and Definitions

This data pack collects FY2025 profitability, cash-generation and capital-expenditure facts for Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA and Apple from the five files of the frozen source pack; it is not an investment opinion. All monetary figures are in US$ billions unless noted, sourced from each company's Form 10-K consolidated statements [S2].

Fiscal-year ends differ by company: Amazon, Alphabet and Meta close in December; Microsoft in June; NVIDIA in late January; Apple in late September. The reference data instructs that companies be compared on a fiscal-year basis, not a calendar basis, so the same fiscal-year label denotes a different twelve-month window for each company [S2]. The Period column in the tables below carries the fiscal-year-end month for that reason [S1].

Metric definitions are those stated in the reference data: operating margin equals operating income divided by revenue; free cash flow (FCF) equals operating cash flow minus capex; capex intensity equals capex divided by operating cash flow [S3]. FCF and the capex/OCF ratio are supplied directly by the sources, so no values are derived in this pack [S3].

Guidance is handled in a separate section. The guidance file is a historical snapshot frozen as of February 7, 2025; it records the first full-year 2025 guidance issued after FY2024 results and states that later revisions must not be substituted and that guidance must remain separate from reported actuals [S61].

## Capex Trends

The reference data provides annual series from FY2020 to FY2025 for each company; the trend below uses the earliest capex value available in the corpus and the FY2025 value, as instructed by the brief [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S47].

- **Amazon (FYE Dec).** Capex rose from 40.1 in FY2020 to 131.8 in FY2025, more than three times the starting level; the capex/OCF ratio moved from 61% to 94% [S42].
- **Alphabet (FYE Dec).** Capex rose from 22.3 in FY2020 to 91.4 in FY2025, roughly a fourfold increase; the capex/OCF ratio moved from 34% to 56% [S43].
- **Meta (FYE Dec).** Capex rose from 15.2 in FY2020 to 69.7 in FY2025, more than four times the starting level; the capex/OCF ratio moved from 39% to 60%, after an intermediate peak of 62% in FY2022 [S44].
- **Microsoft (FYE Jun).** Capex rose from 15.4 in FY2020 to 64.6 in FY2025, roughly a fourfold increase; the capex/OCF ratio moved from 25% to 47% [S45].
- **NVIDIA (FYE Jan).** Capex is recorded as "None" for FY2020 and FY2021, so the earliest available endpoint is FY2022, when capex was 1.0; capex reached 3.2 in FY2025, about three times that level, while the capex/OCF ratio fell from 11% to 5% [S46].
- **Apple (FYE Sep).** Capex rose from 7.3 in FY2020 to 12.7 in FY2025, a moderate increase; the capex/OCF ratio stayed between 8% and 11% across the six years [S47].

The stated bases differ: Amazon's figure is a management estimate of capital expenditures; Alphabet's is reported capex, with cash-payment timing able to cause variability; Meta's includes principal payments on finance leases [S62].

For Meta, the guidance basis and the actual basis differ. The corpus actual is cash paid to acquire property and equipment and excludes finance-lease principal, so the source states that a percentage variance against guidance is not like-for-like and must not be calculated. For reference, Meta's FY2025 company-reported capex measure including finance-lease principal was $72.215B, made up of $69.691B of cash PP&E plus $2.524B of finance-lease principal [S63].

The source pack states explicitly that no initial FY2025 capex guidance is provided for Microsoft, NVIDIA or Apple and that it should be reported as unavailable rather than estimated [S64].

## Cross-Company Comparison

Because fiscal-year ends differ, the comparison below is on a fiscal-year basis and the reporting windows are not aligned in calendar time [S2].

On capex intensity, the six companies fall into three groups. Amazon stands alone at 94% of operating cash flow [S10]. Meta (60%), Alphabet (56%) and Microsoft (47%) form a middle group in which between 47% and 60% of operating cash flow was reinvested in capex [S12][S11][S13]. Apple (11%) and NVIDIA (5%) form a low-intensity group [S15][S14].

On operating margin, NVIDIA reports the highest figure at 62.4% and Amazon the lowest at 11.2% [S14][S10]. Microsoft (45.6%) and Meta (41.4%) sit above Alphabet and Apple, which both report 32.0% [S13][S12][S11][S15].

On free cash flow, the ordering differs from the ordering on revenue or capex: Apple reports the highest FCF at $98.8B, followed by Alphabet ($73.3B), Microsoft ($71.6B), NVIDIA ($60.9B) and Meta ($46.1B), with Amazon lowest at $7.7B [S15][S11][S13][S14][S12][S10].
## Data Gaps

- **FY2025 required actuals.** All seven required metrics are present for all six companies in the key metrics file and are confirmed by the analyst prep notes; no required FY2025 actual is missing [S4][S5][S6][S7][S8][S9][S10][S11][S12][S13][S14][S15].
- **NVIDIA early capex.** The reference data records capex and FCF as "None" for NVIDIA in FY2020 and FY2021; the NVIDIA trend therefore starts at FY2022, and no value is inferred for the missing years [S46].
- **Series start.** The reference data begins in FY2020 for all companies; no earlier capex year is available in the corpus [S42][S43][S44][S45][S46][S47].
- **Guidance coverage.** Initial FY2025 capex guidance is unavailable in the source pack for Microsoft, NVIDIA and Apple [S64].
- **Meta basis mismatch.** The Meta guidance basis includes finance-lease principal while the corpus actual does not; no variance is calculated [S63].
- **Unused file.** The miscellaneous disclosures file was searched and found to contain no content on capex, guidance or the seven required metrics; it contributes no facts to this pack.
