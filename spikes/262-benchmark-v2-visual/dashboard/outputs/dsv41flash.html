Executive view

Four conclusions carry the thesis; the tabs supply the reading.

View 1 — The wave is concentrated and it is not uniform

Amazon is the single largest capital spender in the set — $131.8B FY2025 capex against $139.5B operating cash flow, a 94% capex/OCF ratio — and its 10-K states cash capital expenditures of $128.3B and that both technology infrastructure and fulfillment capacity are expected to increase in 2026. Alphabet ($91.4B, 56%) and Meta ($69.7B, 60%) are the next tier. Microsoft ($64.6B, 47%, FYE Jun) sits below its December-year-end peers on intensity but carries the largest disclosed off-balance-sheet lease pipeline ($92.7B not yet commenced). NVIDIA ($3.2B, 5%) and Apple ($12.7B, 11%) spend a fraction of the others because they are not building hyperscale rented compute at the same scale.

Watch: the gap between the "Big Four" hyperscalers and the two "picks-and-shovels / device" names is the structural feature of the cycle, not a temporary distortion. Where the firm looks next in the value chain should key off which tier is accelerating.

View 2 — Sustainability differs sharply, and the capex line understates the commitment

On the cash-flow line, Amazon converts almost nothing into FCF ($7.7B), Meta and Alphabet retain meaningful FCF ($46.1B, $73.3B), and Apple and NVIDIA are the FCF engines ($98.8B, $60.9B). But the cash-flow capex line is not the whole commitment. Meta discloses $103.77B of leases not yet commenced (mostly data centers) plus $131.05B of contractual commitments; Microsoft discloses $92.7B of not-yet-commenced leases plus $109.95B of purchase commitments; Alphabet discloses $58.5B of not-yet-commenced data-center leases plus $149.1B of purchase commitments. These sit outside the capex line and must be read alongside it.

Watch: free cash flow at Amazon (and, at the margin, Alphabet and Meta) is a residual after an investment program that is still ramping — treat FCF as a policy variable, not a steady-state earnings proxy.

View 3 — Guidance is being kept, but the definitions are not the same

Initial FY2025 guidance exists only for Amazon (~$100B), Alphabet (~$75B) and Meta ($60B–$65B); Microsoft, NVIDIA and Apple did not give initial FY2025 capex guidance in the pack and are marked not disclosed. Amazon and Alphabet actuals came in above their initial guidance. Meta's actuals are not like-for-like against its guidance: the cash-flow line ($69.691B) excludes finance-lease principal, while guidance included it; Meta's own measure including principal is $72.215B. No variance should be computed against Meta's guidance.

Watch: the 2026 indications (Alphabet "significantly increase", Meta $115B–$135B, Amazon "increase", NVIDIA "increase") are forward-looking and are kept strictly apart from FY2025 actuals throughout this dashboard.

View 4 — The 10-Ks read as accelerating, with a supply/energy constraint layer

Every hyperscaler's 10-K language points to acceleration: Alphabet's "significantly increase" in 2026, Meta's $115B–$135B 2026 range, Amazon's "expect to increase in 2026", Microsoft's continued investment in AI infrastructure. The offsetting signals are capacity, not demand: land, power, water, GPU supply and lead times. NVIDIA's own demand commentary is a supply-side read on the same cycle. The cycle reads as accelerating on committed spend and slowing only where the constraint is physical.

Watch: depreciation policy is the quiet lever. Amazon shortened server/network lives from six to five years (a $1.4B depreciation increase in FY2025); Meta extended most servers/network assets to 5.5 years (a $2.92B depreciation reduction). Useful-life choices materially change reported margins and are a signal of management's view of asset obsolescence.

Capex intensity vs FCF — the trade-off

Reading: the upper-left is the "spend-heavy, FCF-light" zone (Amazon); the lower-right is the "cash-rich, capex-light" zone (Apple, NVIDIA). Alphabet and Meta sit in the middle; Microsoft is capex-heavier than its June year-end peers on a like-for-like intensity basis.

What each company's FCF actually is

  • Amazon — $7.7B FCF at 94% intensity. The capex line is dominated by technology infrastructure supporting AWS; the 10-K states cash capital expenditures of $128.3B in 2025 and expects technology infrastructure and fulfillment capacity to increase in 2026. FCF here is a policy residual, not a steady-state number.
  • Alphabet — $73.3B FCF at 56%. Capex $91.4B; the 10-K says 2026 investment in technical infrastructure will "significantly increase" relative to 2025. FCF is meaningful but is the first line to compress if the 2026 step-up lands as guided.
  • Meta — $46.1B FCF at 60%. Capex $69.7B on the cash-flow line; company measure including finance-lease principal is $72.215B. FCF is real but the 2026 range ($115B–$135B) implies a further step-down in FCF unless OCF grows faster.
  • Microsoft — $71.6B FCF at 47% (FYE Jun). Capex $64.6B; the 10-K flags continued investment in cloud and AI infrastructure and that this "will continue to increase our operating costs and may decrease our operating margins".
  • NVIDIA — $60.9B FCF at 5%. Capex $3.2B; the company is a supplier to the cycle, not a builder of rented compute at hyperscale. FCF is high and the 10-K flags that FY2026 capex will increase relative to FY2025.
  • Apple — $98.8B FCF at 11%. Capex $12.7B; the 10-K makes no AI/data-center investment strategy statement. The largest FCF in the set and the least exposed to the capex wave on the reported numbers.
Definition caveat (Meta): the data-pack actual for Meta is cash paid to acquire property and equipment ($69.691B), which excludes finance-lease principal. Meta's own FY2025 capex measure including principal payments on finance leases is $72.215B ($69.691B cash PP&E + $2.524B finance-lease principal). Guidance included principal; the cash-flow line does not. Do not compute a variance against guidance.

Why this matters for the thesis

  • Off-line commitments are contractual. Leases not yet commenced and non-cancelable purchase obligations are commitments the company has already made; they will show up as capex or lease expense in future periods.
  • They change the risk profile. A company with a large not-yet-commenced lease book has less flexibility to slow the cycle than one whose commitment is mostly cancellable purchase orders.
  • They are unevenly disclosed. Meta and Microsoft quantify not-yet-commenced leases; Amazon and Alphabet quantify purchase commitments more fully; Apple does not break out data-center-specific amounts. Treat cross-company totals as directional, not like-for-like.

10-K signals — is the cycle accelerating or slowing?

Each company's FY2025 10-K read for direction. "Accelerating" = language and commitments point to higher spend ahead; "slowing" = language points to constraint or moderation; "mixed" = both present.

Signal detail by company

Amazon — accelerating. 10-K: cash capex $128.3B in 2025; tech infrastructure (majority AWS) and fulfillment capacity "both of which we expect to increase in 2026"; "we expect to continue making additional investments in our artificial intelligence initiatives". Constraint layer: limited group of semiconductor suppliers incl. GPUs; energy contracts ~200M MWh with ~16-yr weighted average remaining duration. Watch: AWS segment net additions to PP&E rose from $53.267B (2024) to $96.496B (2025).
Alphabet — accelerating. 10-K: "in 2026, we expect to significantly increase, relative to 2025, our investment in our technical infrastructure, including servers and network equipment, and data centers". Constraint layer: "ability to scale our technical infrastructure is increasingly constrained by the availability of power, water, and land"; specialized AI chips limited to a small number of qualified suppliers. Watch: $149.1B purchase commitments, $113.0B short-term; Jan-2026 PPA expected to be a lease, $9.9B 2027–2047.
Meta — accelerating. 10-K: 2026 capex $115B–$135B "to support our AI efforts and core business"; "we expect our AI initiatives will require significantly increased investment in infrastructure". Constraint layer: supply-chain challenges; reliance on third-party equipment/components from a small number of suppliers, often concentrated in Asia; power/network capacity cited as project risk. Watch: $103.77B leases not yet commenced; $131.05B contractual commitments; $30.0B notes issued Nov-2025.
Microsoft — accelerating. 10-K: "We will continue to invest in capital expenditures to support growth in our cloud offerings and our investments in AI infrastructure and training"; investments "will continue to increase our operating costs and may decrease our operating margins". Constraint layer: datacenters depend on permitted/buildable land, predictable energy, networking supplies, servers incl. GPUs; limited suppliers for certain datacenter components. Watch: $92.7B leases not yet commenced; $109.953B purchase commitments; $32.149B construction commitments.
NVIDIA — accelerating (as supplier). 10-K: "We expect to increase capital expenditures in fiscal year 2026 relative to fiscal year 2025 to support the future growth of our business"; Data Center revenue up 142% year on year; Blackwell production shipping in Q4 FY2025. Constraint layer: long manufacturing lead times (some >12 months); CoWoS packaging; supply chain concentrated in Asia-Pacific. Watch: $30.8B inventory/supply obligations; $14.3B other purchase obligations incl. $10.9B multi-year cloud agreements.
Apple — not disclosed / mixed. 10-K: no AI or data-center infrastructure investment strategy statement; data centers named only as a category of owned/leased facilities. Capex $12.7B; other purchase obligations $14.8B ($7.0B within 12 months); fixed lease payments $16.8B ($2.6B within 12 months). AI appears only in risk factors (product features, IP, regulation). Watch: absence of a stated AI-infrastructure strategy is itself a signal — Apple is not competing on owned hyperscale compute at the scale of the other five.

Cross-cutting signals

  • Accelerating: all four hyperscalers use forward language pointing to higher 2026 spend; NVIDIA guides FY2026 capex up. The direction of travel is up across the set.
  • Slowing / constraining: the binding constraints named in the 10-Ks are physical — land, power, water, GPU supply, qualified suppliers, lead times — not demand. This is a supply-side brake on an accelerating cycle, not a demand slowdown.
  • Financing: Alphabet raised debt in 2025 and expects to continue assessing debt and other financing; Meta issued $30.0B of notes in Nov-2025; Amazon and Alphabet provide credit support/backstops to infrastructure counterparties. The cycle is increasingly debt- and lease-financed, which raises the sensitivity to rates and counterparty risk.
  • Depreciation: Amazon shortened server lives; Meta extended them. The two moves are a useful tell on how each management team views AI-era asset obsolescence.

Named risks and where the firm should look next

Risks are drawn from the 10-K extracts and the data pack; the "look next" list follows from the readings above and is a view, not a recommendation.

Risks

  • Definition risk (Meta). Cash-flow capex excludes finance-lease principal; guidance included it. Cross-company capex comparisons that ignore this understate Meta's commitment by ~$2.5B in FY2025 and more in forward periods.
  • Off-line commitment risk. Meta ($103.77B), Microsoft ($92.7B) and Alphabet ($58.5B) carry large not-yet-commenced lease pipelines; Amazon ($96.373B) and Alphabet ($149.1B) carry large purchase commitments. These are contractual and reduce flexibility to slow the cycle.
  • Depreciation-policy risk. Amazon's shortening and Meta's extension move reported earnings in opposite directions on similar assets; margin comparisons across the set need adjustment.
  • Supply and energy constraint risk. Every hyperscaler names land, power, water, GPU supply or qualified-supplier concentration as a constraint. A constraint on the supply side is a brake on revenue conversion even while capex runs.
  • Financing risk. Debt issuance (Meta $30.0B; Alphabet debt in 2025), backstops and credit derivatives (Alphabet $5.7B guarantees, $16.9B credit derivatives) and residual value guarantees (Meta ~$28B threshold) increase sensitivity to rates and counterparties.
  • Demand-forecast risk. NVIDIA explicitly flags over/under-estimation of demand and non-cancellable purchase orders; Amazon flags data-center capacity risk; Meta flags impairment/abandonment risk on data-center and infrastructure assets.
  • Disclosure risk. Apple does not disclose a data-center-specific lease or commitment amount, nor an AI-infrastructure strategy. Absence of disclosure is not absence of exposure, but it cannot be sized from the pack.

Where the firm should look next in the value chain

  • Power and energy. The binding constraint named across the hyperscalers is power, water and land. Amazon's ~200M MWh energy contracts with ~16-yr duration and Alphabet's Jan-2026 PPA expected to be a $9.9B lease (2027–2047) are early evidence of long-dated energy contracting. This is the most consistently named constraint in the set.
  • Data-center leasing and build-to-suit. Meta's Louisiana Venture (20% equity interest, ~$27B total development cost, ~$12.31B lease commitment, ~$28B RVG threshold) and Alphabet's Intersect acquisition ($4.8B pending) show hyperscalers moving into the landlord/developer layer. The not-yet-commenced lease books ($103.77B Meta, $92.7B Microsoft, $58.5B Alphabet, $96.373B Amazon) are the addressable market.
  • Custom silicon and networking. Alphabet's TPU program (Ironwood, seventh-gen) and Microsoft's custom-built silicon are explicit in the 10-Ks; NVIDIA's moat is the counter-position. The custom-silicon vs merchant-GPU split is where the margin pool sits.
  • Financing and credit support. Alphabet's backstops ($5.7B guarantees, $16.9B credit derivatives) and Meta's $30.0B notes issuance are early signs of a financing layer forming around the cycle. This is a place to look for fee pools and counterparty risk.
  • Depreciation and asset-life analytics. The Amazon/Meta divergence on server lives is a small but clean signal that asset-obsolescence assumptions are becoming a competitive and accounting variable. A firm view on AI-era asset lives is an input to any infrastructure underwriting.
Bottom line for the MP: the capex wave is real, concentrated in four hyperscalers, and larger than the capex line shows once leases-not-yet-commenced and purchase commitments are added. Sustainability differs sharply — Amazon is the stress case, Apple and NVIDIA the cash engines — and the binding constraint is physical supply, not demand. The most defensible next look is the power/energy and data-center-leasing layer, with custom silicon and the emerging financing layer as secondary.
