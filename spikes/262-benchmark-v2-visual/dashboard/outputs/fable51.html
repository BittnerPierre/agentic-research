Executive summary

The wave is concentrated in four companies, it overshot every initial FY2025 guide that exists, it is financed increasingly outside the capex line, and nothing in the 10-Ks signals a slowdown for 2026. The sustainability question is now Amazon-specific; the risk questions are shared.

Takeaways for the partners' meeting

  1. Four spenders, two bystanders. Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft reported FY2025 capex of $131.8B, $91.4B, $69.7B and $64.6B on the cash-flow line. NVIDIA ($3.2B) and Apple ($12.7B) are not capex stories: NVIDIA is the supplier whose revenue is the other side of this ledger, and Apple's 10-K discloses no AI-infrastructure investment strategy at all.
  2. Intensity has roughly doubled since FY2020, and Amazon has reached the limit. Capex absorbed 94% of Amazon's operating cash flow (61% in FY2020), leaving $7.7B of free cash flow. Meta (60%), Alphabet (56%) and Microsoft (47%) still convert 40 to 53% of operating cash flow to free cash flow, which is why their programmes read as sustainable and Amazon's reads as a choice to run at the edge.
  3. Every initial FY2025 guide was exceeded. Amazon guided to approximately $100B and spent $128.3B on its own cash-capex basis ($131.8B on the cash-flow line). Alphabet guided to approximately $75B and spent $91.4B. Meta guided to $60B to $65B including finance-lease principal and reported $72.2B on that same basis. Microsoft, NVIDIA and Apple issued no initial FY2025 capex guidance in the source pack.
  4. The commitment outside the capex line is as large as the capex line. Leases not yet commenced stand at $103.8B (Meta), $96.4B (Amazon), $92.7B (Microsoft) and $58.5B (Alphabet). Non-cancellable purchase commitments add $149.1B (Alphabet), $131.0B (Meta), $110.0B (Microsoft) and $84.8B (Amazon). These are stated on each company's own basis and are not additive across companies, but their order of magnitude is the point.
  5. The 2026 direction is up everywhere it is disclosed. Meta quantifies $115B to $135B. Alphabet expects to "significantly increase" relative to 2025. Amazon expects technology-infrastructure and fulfilment capex "to increase in 2026". NVIDIA expects higher capex in FY2026. Microsoft gives no number. Apple gives nothing.
  6. The signals of strain are in accounting and financing, not in spending. Amazon shortened server lives (six to five years, citing the pace of AI), while Meta lengthened them (to 5.5 years, lifting net income by $2.59B). Microsoft's cloud gross margin fell to 69% from scaling AI infrastructure. Meta issued $30B of notes; Alphabet raised debt and wrote $22.6B of backstops and credit derivatives (computed sum) for third-party data-centre and power counterparties. Meta states it has "changed, suspended, and terminated certain of these projects".
  7. Where to look next. The binding constraints named in the filings are power, water, land, specialised chips and memory, and the lease and financing capacity of third-party data-centre operators. That is the value chain the firm should map: energy procurement and generation partners, data-centre developers and lessors, memory and packaging supply, and the financing structures (backstops, residual-value guarantees, joint ventures) that now carry part of the build.

2Trajectory: FY2020 to FY2025

The data pack gives the start and end of each series; the 10-Ks add FY2023 and FY2024 on their own cash-flow lines. Capex has multiplied three- to five-fold at the four spenders, while intensity has risen by 21 to 33 points.

Figure 2. From first available year to FY2025
Left endpoint is FY2020 for five companies and FY2022 for NVIDIA (capex recorded as "None" for FY2020 and FY2021). Intermediate years are not in the data pack and are not drawn. Multiples and point changes are computed from the two endpoints.
What it showsMeta (4.6x), Microsoft (4.2x) and Alphabet (4.1x) have multiplied capex by more than Amazon (3.3x), but Amazon started from a base twice the size and ends at $131.8B. Apple's 1.7x and NVIDIA's 3.2x from a $1.0B base are a different order of magnitude. Intensity: Amazon +33 points, Microsoft +22, Alphabet +22, Meta +21 (after a 62% peak in FY2022), Apple flat at 8 to 11%, NVIDIA down to 5%.
Why it mattersThe intensity line, not the dollar line, is the sustainability test. A fourfold capex increase that still leaves half of operating cash flow as free cash flow (Microsoft, Alphabet) is a different proposition from one funded at the expense of free cash flow (Amazon).
What to watchMeta's FY2022 intensity peak of 62% preceded a lower ratio before the current acceleration. A repeat of that pattern would be the first quantitative sign of a slowing cycle; the FY2025 ratio of 60% is already back near that level.

3Sustainability: what operating cash flow is left after capex

Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capex on the cash-flow line, as defined in the data pack. The ordering on free cash flow bears no resemblance to the ordering on capex, which is the point of this view.

Figure 3. FY2025 operating cash flow split into capex and free cash flow
US$ billions. Bar length is operating cash flow; the darker segment is capex, the lighter segment is free cash flow. Hover for percentages.
What it showsApple generates the most free cash flow ($98.8B) while spending the second-least. Alphabet ($73.3B) and Microsoft ($71.6B) fund programmes of $91.4B and $64.6B and still retain more free cash than NVIDIA. Meta retains $46.1B. Amazon retains $7.7B from $139.5B of operating cash flow.
Why it mattersThe four spenders differ in how far their programmes depend on continued revenue growth. Alphabet and Microsoft could absorb a flat year. Meta has already chosen to supplement cash flow with $30B of notes. Amazon's 10-K says explicitly that infrastructure spending "can negatively impact short-term free cash flow", and the number confirms it.
What to watchOperating margin is the second leg: NVIDIA 62.4%, Microsoft 45.6%, Meta 41.4%, Alphabet and Apple 32.0%, Amazon 11.2%. Alphabet and Microsoft both warn in their 10-Ks of downward margin pressure from infrastructure costs; depreciation is already rising 18 to 45% a year at the four spenders (Section 6).

6Signals in the 10-Ks: is the cycle accelerating or slowing?

Read together, the six filings give one accelerating message on spending and a set of quieter signals on cost, accounting and financing that say the cycle is getting more expensive to sustain. The matrix below classifies each disclosed signal; the depreciation view quantifies the cost already flowing through.

US$ billions, each company's disclosed depreciation measure (captions differ; see tooltip). The multiple is computed as FY2025 cash-flow-line capex divided by FY2025 depreciation.
What it showsDepreciation rose 31% at Amazon ($32.1B to $41.9B on property and equipment), 38% at Alphabet ($15.3B to $21.1B), 18% at Meta ($15.29B to $18.00B, after the useful-life extension), 45% at Microsoft ($15.2B to $22.0B); all computed. Capex runs at 2.9x (Microsoft) to 4.3x (Alphabet) current depreciation at the four spenders.
Why it mattersA capex-to-depreciation multiple of three to four means the depreciation charge will keep stepping up for years even if capex stopped growing. Alphabet says so directly: infrastructure operating costs "are expected to significantly increase". Microsoft's cloud gross margin fell to 69% for this reason. This is the margin cost of the cycle, and it lags the cash by the asset life.
What to watchUseful-life policy. Amazon cut a subset of servers from six to five years because of AI's pace, adding $1.4B of expense; Meta extended most servers to 5.5 years, removing $2.92B. Alphabet holds at six years and says it reviews for obsolescence. Convergence toward Amazon's view would raise the group's reported cost of the cycle; a further extension would flatter it.

7Conclusions, risks and where to look next in the value chain

Conclusions

The cycle is accelerating into 2026 on every disclosed measure. Four companies spent $357.5B on the cash-flow line in FY2025 (computed sum across unaligned fiscal years), overshot the three guides that existed by 20 to 30%, contracted a further ~$351B of leases not yet commenced and ~$475B of purchase commitments (computed sums, differing bases), and all indicate higher 2026 spend. Meta alone guides to $115B to $135B.

Sustainability is a two-tier question. Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta retain $46B to $73B of free cash flow and 32 to 46% operating margins; they can fund the next leg from operations, and Meta has pre-funded with $30B of notes. Amazon has chosen to run at 94% of operating cash flow with an 11.2% margin. Its programme is the largest and the most dependent on AWS growth continuing to outrun the depreciation it is creating.

The definition of capex now matters to the number. Amazon's own measure is $3.5B below the cash-flow line; Meta's is $2.5B above; NVIDIA's MD&A figure is $0.2B above a cash-flow caption that includes intangibles. Finance leases (Microsoft's liabilities rose from $27.1B to $46.2B) and leases not yet commenced carry spend the capex line never shows. Any cross-company view that uses one line understates the contracted build by roughly a year's worth of capex at each of the four spenders.

NVIDIA's and Apple's places are different. NVIDIA's $30.8B of supply and capacity obligations, up for Blackwell, is the supplier-side confirmation of the same cycle; its stated demand risk is "our customers' ability to invest in AI infrastructure", which makes it the most levered name to a slowdown. Apple's 10-K is silent on AI infrastructure investment; it is neither a driver nor, on this evidence, a participant.

Named risks

Return risk. Alphabet: investments "may not be commercially viable or may not result in an adequate return of capital". Amazon: failure to realise benefits "could result in the value of those investments being written down or written off". Meta: "If our investments are not successful longer-term, our business and financial performance will be harmed." Microsoft: new services "may not achieve operating margins as high as we have experienced historically".
Stranded-capacity and counterparty risk. Alphabet: in "nonperformance or an industry downturn, we may incur additional liabilities, have excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy". Meta: "charges associated with impairment or abandonment of any assets ... including ... data center and infrastructure assets"; projects already "changed, suspended, and terminated".
Obsolescence risk, now visible in accounting. Amazon shortened server lives for AI; Alphabet warns that "expected technology advancements ... could change the period over which we expect to benefit from the asset". Meta's extension to 5.5 years runs the other way and adds $2.59B to net income; if it reverses, the charge reverses.
Margin risk. Microsoft Cloud gross margin down to 69% "driven by the impact of scaling our AI infrastructure"; Alphabet expects infrastructure costs to "significantly increase" and names downward margin pressure; Meta's cost of revenue up 20% and R&D up 31%.
Demand-side risk at the supplier. NVIDIA lists "our customers' ability to invest in AI infrastructure" and "the availability of sufficient data center capacity or energy for customers to procure" among the factors that cause it to over- or under-estimate demand, with non-cancellable orders amplifying any miss.
Financing risk. Meta: additional debt "results in increased fixed obligations and interest expense"; financing "may not be available on favorable terms or at all". Alphabet: leases, backstops and guarantees "could harm our financial condition and reduce our financial and business flexibility".

What to watch over the next four quarters

Meta's $115B to $135B. The only quantified 2026 guide. A move above the range, or a basis change, is the first read on whether 2026 repeats 2025's overshoot pattern.
Amazon's intensity and unpaid additions. Capex/OCF of 94% and $27.0B of property acquired but not yet paid. Either rising further means free cash flow on the data-pack definition turns negative; the 10-K already warns of the short-term effect.
Lease commencements. ~$351B of not-yet-commenced leases start converting to balance-sheet liabilities from 2026. The conversion rate, and any cancellations, will show whether third-party build is on schedule.
Useful-life decisions. Any further change at Amazon, Alphabet or Microsoft (computer equipment two to six years, no change disclosed) resets the margin cost of the installed base.
Backstops and guarantees. Alphabet's $22.6B and Meta's ~$28B residual-value threshold are off-balance-sheet today ("not probable" at Meta). Their first recognition would signal counterparty stress in the developer layer.
Microsoft's next 10-K (FYE June 2026). The only spender without a 2026 number. Its FY2026 cash-flow line, finance-lease additions and the $103.9B of purchase commitments falling due in FY2026 close the gap between its June year-end and the December filers.

A2. Definitions and handling rules

Operating margin = operating income / revenue. Free cash flow = operating cash flow minus capex. Capex intensity = capex / operating cash flow. All three are supplied by the data pack; nothing is re-derived except where a figure is marked "computed", in which case the inputs are the ones displayed beside it.

Capex (cash-flow line): the data pack's capex figure, which corresponds to each company's "purchases of" or "additions to" property and equipment line in the consolidated cash-flow statement. Company's own measure: a capex figure the company states in MD&A on a different basis (Amazon's "cash capital expenditures", Meta's capex "including principal payments on finance leases", NVIDIA's MD&A total). Leases not yet commenced: signed leases whose term has not started, disclosed as future payments or total obligations on each company's basis. Purchase / contractual commitments: non-cancellable obligations as each company defines them.

Fiscal years. All comparisons are on the fiscal-year label. FY2025 covers calendar 2025 for Amazon, Alphabet and Meta; July 2024 to June 2025 for Microsoft; late January 2024 to 26 January 2025 for NVIDIA; late September 2024 to 27 September 2025 for Apple. The NVIDIA 10-K was filed in February 2025 and the Microsoft 10-K in July 2025, so their forward-looking statements are older than those of the December filers (January and February 2026).

Guidance. Initial FY2025 capex guidance is the first full-year guide issued after FY2024 results, frozen as of 7 February 2025. Later revisions are not used. 2026 indications are taken only from the FY2025 10-Ks. Where the pack states guidance is unavailable, it is shown as unavailable.
