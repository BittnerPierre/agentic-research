Investment conclusions

  1. Physical infrastructure remains an accelerating demand pool. Amazon expects technology infrastructure and fulfillment investment to rise in 2026; Alphabet expects technical-infrastructure investment to increase significantly; Meta guides to $115–135bn; Microsoft and NVIDIA indicate continued or higher investment. Apple gives no capex trajectory.
  2. Amazon carries the tightest cash-funding profile. Its $131.8bn cash-flow capex consumed 94% of $139.5bn OCF, leaving $7.7bn of dashboard-defined FCF. Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft consumed 47–60% and retained $46.1–73.3bn.
  3. The capex line understates economic commitment. Large leases not yet commenced and purchase or construction commitments sit outside current-period cash capex. These categories differ and may overlap; they should not be mechanically summed.
  4. Demand broadens beyond GPUs. Filings repeatedly identify power, water, land, permitting, networking, memory, specialized chips, construction labor, cloud capacity and long-lead equipment as constraints.
  5. Accounting policy is becoming an earnings variable. Amazon shortened useful lives for a subset of servers; Meta extended most server and network lives. These changes moved FY2025 depreciation and net income in opposite directions.
Sources: quantitative data pack [S1–S7]; FY2025 10-K extracts T1–T7.

Where to look next in the value chain

  • Power and grid-enablement: global energy availability is explicitly constrained; contracts extend for years or decades.
  • Data-center development and financing: large not-yet-commenced leases, construction commitments, guarantees and residual-value structures indicate continued use of third-party capital.
  • Networking, memory and packaging: the constraint set extends beyond accelerators to networking supplies, memory chips, CoWoS packaging and specialized components.
  • Cooling, water and efficiency: higher compute density and constrained power/water increase the value of infrastructure efficiency.

Principal thesis risks

  • Demand or monetization fails to justify fixed capacity, leading to underutilization, impairment or margin compression.
  • Useful-life assumptions defer or accelerate reported costs without changing cash economics.
  • Supply, power or permitting bottlenecks delay revenue conversion even when commitments remain binding.
  • Fiscal-year misalignment creates false cross-company timing comparisons.

Definition risk

A single “capex” ranking can mix cash PP&E, company-defined capital expenditures, finance-lease principal and purchases that include intangible assets. This dashboard does not normalize beyond the source basis and labels each exception explicitly.

Takeaway and risk

Amazon and Alphabet materially exceeded their initial indications, while Meta’s initial range cannot be tested against the common cash PP&E line. The pattern supports acceleration, but not necessarily weak control: timing of equipment payments and capacity delivery can move annual capex. The key 2026 test is whether capacity additions translate into revenue before depreciation, lease and energy costs pressure margins.

Signals from the FY2025 10-Ks

Direction of travel, evidence and the investment implication. “Accelerating” refers to filing language, not a forecast generated by this dashboard.

Amazon

FYE 31 Dec. 2025
Accelerating

Technology infrastructure and fulfillment capacity are expected to increase in 2026. The majority of cash capex supports AWS growth, while AI and machine-learning investment is expected to continue.

We expect spending in technology and infrastructure to increase over time.
Watch: 94% cash absorption, GPU and memory availability, capacity optimization and the shorter five-year life for a subset of servers.

Alphabet

FYE 31 Dec. 2025
Accelerating

Alphabet explicitly expects a significant 2026 increase in servers, network equipment and data centers. AI workloads require more compute than historical offerings.

In 2026, we expect to significantly increase, relative to 2025, our investment in our technical infrastructure.
Watch: power, water and land constraints; $149.1bn commitments; margin pressure; lease and credit-support exposure.

Meta

FYE 31 Dec. 2025
Step-up quantified

Meta guides to $115–135bn for 2026 and says generative AI and superintelligence require significantly more infrastructure. The commitment stack is already large.

We anticipate making capital expenditures of approximately $115 billion to $135 billion in 2026.
Watch: monetization, third-party cloud dependence, $103.77bn future leases, and the earnings effect of longer server lives.

Microsoft

FYE 30 June 2025
Continuing

Microsoft plans continued cloud and AI infrastructure investment. FY2025 already showed gross-margin pressure from scaling AI infrastructure despite Azure efficiency gains.

We will continue to invest in capital expenditures to support growth in our cloud offerings and our investments in AI infrastructure and training.
Watch: AI utilization, power and GPU access, $92.7bn future leases, and whether efficiency offsets depreciation and operating costs.

NVIDIA

FYE 26 Jan. 2025
Demand strong

Data Center revenue rose 142% and Blackwell systems began shipping in Q4. NVIDIA expects capex to increase, but its larger economic commitments are in supply capacity and cloud services.

We expect to increase capital expenditures in fiscal year 2026 relative to fiscal year 2025.
Watch: customer power and data-center capacity, CoWoS and memory supply, long lead times, inventory commitments and demand forecasting.

Apple

FYE 27 Sept. 2025
No trajectory disclosed

The filing does not state an AI or data-center infrastructure investment strategy or forward capex trajectory. R&D growth included infrastructure costs, but no AI-specific amount is provided.

The growth in R&D expense…was primarily driven by increases in headcount-related expenses and infrastructure-related costs.
Watch: whether future filings identify Private Cloud Compute or AI infrastructure separately; current lease and purchase totals are mixed-purpose.

Method, comparability and source discipline

Designed to preserve the basis supplied by the quantitative report and each FY2025 10-K extract.

Core conventions

  • All monetary amounts are US$ billions unless stated otherwise.
  • Capex for the common comparison is the quantitative pack’s purchases of property and equipment measure.
  • FCF = OCF − capex. Capex intensity = capex / OCF.
  • Initial FY2025 guidance, FY2025 actuals and FY2026 indications are separate.
  • No missing guidance or commitment amount is estimated; it is shown as “not disclosed.”
