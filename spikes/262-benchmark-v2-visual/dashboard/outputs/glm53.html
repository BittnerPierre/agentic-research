Six Balance Sheets, One Build-Out

The FY2025 capex wave at Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA and Apple — what was spent, how it was funded, what is committed off the capex line, and what the six 10-Ks say about where the cycle goes next. ≈15-minute read.

For the Managing PartnerThe read in five lines

Everything below comes from two inputs only — the firm’s quantitative data pack (FY2020–FY2025, XBRL) and the six FY2025 10-K extract files. Aggregations and percentages computed on disclosed figures are marked as our arithmetic; everything else is reported. Gaps are reported as “not disclosed.”

1

The language is one-way.

All five spenders lifted capex sharply in FY2025 on their own reported bases — Meta +87% (cash basis), Alphabet +74%, Amazon +65% (MD&A basis), Microsoft +45%, NVIDIA ≈3× — and all five state in their 10-Ks that FY2026 rises further. Only Meta quantifies the step: $115–135B. Apple grew +35% too, but from $9.4B, and its 10-K discloses no AI-infrastructure investment strategy at all.

2

$373.4B in one fiscal year — on six different clocks.

Six-company FY2025 capex (our addition across fiscal calendars) against $100.3B for the same five companies in FY2020 — ≈3.7× in five years. Amazon alone is $131.8B, 35% of the group; the December-year three (AMZN, GOOGL, META) are ≈78%.

3

Funding holds — with one stretched name and a new marginal lender.

Amazon ran at 94% of operating cash flow (FCF $7.7B), below the 132–136% peak intensity of FY2021–22, when FCF turned negative. Meta (60%) and Alphabet (56%) now commit the majority of OCF; the marginal dollar is turning to debt and leases — Meta issued $30.0B of notes in November 2025, Alphabet raised debt in 2025 and expects to continue finance leases “primarily for data centers,” and Microsoft’s finance-lease liabilities rose 70% in a year.

4

Initial guidance was overtaken.

The pack’s two initial FY2025 guides were beaten by ≈+32% (Amazon: ~$100B → $131.8B) and ≈+22% (Alphabet: ~$75B → $91.4B) — our arithmetic. Meta’s like-for-like reference measure ($72.2B incl. finance-lease principal) finished above its $60–65B initial band; on the pack’s cash basis no variance is calculated. Treat the 2026 indications as floors, not ceilings.

5

The bigger commitment sits off the capex line.

Leases not yet commenced total $358.9B at the five companies that disclose them — ≈ the same size as those five companies’ entire FY2025 cash capex ($360.7B) — plus purchase and construction commitments from $14.8B (Apple) to $149.1B (Alphabet). And depreciation is the next margin story: PP&E D&A rose +31% at Amazon, +45% at Microsoft, +38% at Alphabet, while the two heaviest spenders moved server useful lives in opposite directions in 2025.

01 · Who is spendingScale, clocks and concentration

FY2025 capex spans $3.2B (NVIDIA) to $131.8B (Amazon) — a 41× spread — and intensity spans 5% to 94% of operating cash flow. Those are different questions: who spends the most, and who spends relative to what they generate. Amazon, Alphabet and Meta alone account for $292.9B, ≈78% of the six-company total. The money is visible on balance sheets: AWS net additions to property and equipment were $96.5B in 2025 (2024: $53.3B) and AWS net PP&E reached $190.1B; Meta’s servers and network assets rose to $98.0B (from $68.4B) with construction in progress at $50.5B (from $26.8B); Microsoft’s computer equipment and software reached $132.8B (from $93.8B).

Which definition of capex. Throughout, capex is each company’s cash-flow-statement line unless flagged. Three companies maintain a second, different definition, and both are named wherever it matters: Amazon (cash-flow line $131.8B vs MD&A “cash capital expenditures” $128.3B), Meta (cash purchases of P&E $69.7B vs company-reported $72.2B including finance-lease principal), and NVIDIA (cash-flow line, including intangibles, $3.2B vs MD&A’s $3.4B).

02 · How sustainablyFunding the wave

The pack’s definitions: FCF = OCF − capex; intensity = capex ÷ OCF. Three of the four builders now commit a majority of operating cash to capex. Amazon retains $7.7B of FCF — the group’s thinnest buffer — though its 94% intensity remains below the 132–136% it ran in FY2021–22, when FCF was negative. The new signal is financing at the margin: Meta issued $30.0B of notes in November 2025 and carries $103.8B of leases not yet commenced; Alphabet raised debt in 2025, expects to continue finance leases “primarily for data centers,” and has extended $22.6B of backstops ($16.9B credit derivatives on third-party data-center leases plus $5.7B of guarantees) to infrastructure counterparties; Microsoft’s finance-lease liabilities rose from $27.1B to $46.2B in a year, while stating its cash, investments, OCF and capital-markets access remain sufficient for at least the next twelve months.

03 · The trajectoryFive years, and the latest step

On the data pack’s five-year view, Meta multiplied capex 4.6×, Microsoft 4.2×, Alphabet 4.1×, Amazon 3.3× and Apple 1.7×; NVIDIA, whose series begins FY2022, 3.2×. The more telling line is the latest year, on each company’s own 10-K basis: +87% (Meta, cash basis), +74% (Alphabet), +65% (Amazon, MD&A basis), +45% (Microsoft), ≈3× (NVIDIA) and +35% (Apple, from $9.4B). There is no deceleration in the reported numbers; the only company not visibly accelerating is the one not participating.

04 · Guidance vs actualsDid they keep guidance — and what do they say about 2026?

The pack freezes initial FY2025 guidance as of 7 February 2025 (the first full-year 2025 guidance available after FY2024 results) and keeps it strictly separate from actuals and from 2026 indications. Only three companies guided. Amazon (~$100B, 6 Feb 2025) and Alphabet (~$75B, 4 Feb 2025) finished ≈+32% and ≈+22% above their initial guides — our arithmetic on disclosed figures. Meta guided $60–65B including finance-lease principal; its cash-basis actual ($69.7B) is not like-for-like, so the pack calculates no variance. On the company’s own reported basis, FY2025 capex was $72.215B ($69.691B cash P&E + $2.524B finance-lease principal) — above the initial band. Microsoft, NVIDIA and Apple gave no initial FY2025 capex guidance; that is reported as not disclosed, not inferred.

05 · Beyond the capex lineThe contracted pipeline

Capex is the spent line; the commitment increasingly sits ahead of it, in three disclosed places: leases not yet commenced, purchase and construction commitments, and — smaller but telling — equipment delivered but not yet paid. The five companies that disclose leases not yet commenced carry $358.9B of them: Meta $103.8B, Amazon $96.4B, Microsoft $92.7B, Alphabet $58.5B, NVIDIA $7.6B. Apple does not disclose the figure. Bases vary and purchase obligations are whole-company — content licensing at Amazon and Alphabet, third-party cloud capacity at Meta — so read the shape, not the decimal.

Takeaway

Whoever finances the not-yet-commenced lease book — and whoever guarantees residual values — is financing this cycle. That is a value-chain question as much as a credit question.

06 · 10-K signalsAccelerating or slowing?

Verdict first: five accelerate, one abstains. Alphabet uses the strongest language and is the only one buying the constraint outright — the $4.8B Intersect acquisition (data-center and energy infrastructure, closing H1 2026) and a January 2026 power purchase agreement worth $9.9B between 2027 and 2047. Meta is the only one to quantify 2026. Microsoft points to $92.7B of not-yet-commenced leases and finance leases that grew 70% in a year, and concedes its cloud/AI investments “will continue to increase our operating costs and may decrease our operating margins” — Microsoft Cloud gross margin already fell to 69%, “driven by the impact of scaling our AI infrastructure.” Amazon expects capex to increase in 2026 and shortened server lives, accepting faster obsolescence. NVIDIA expects FY2026 capex up and holds $30.8B of supply commitments. Apple’s 10-K, by contrast, discloses no AI or data-center investment strategy at all — its only AI mentions are risk factors.

07 · Named risksWhat the filings themselves flag

Six risks, each named by the companies in their own Item 1A and liquidity disclosures, with the figures that anchor them.

1

Power, land and water

The binding input of the cycle, named by four of six. Alphabet says scaling technical infrastructure is “increasingly constrained by the availability of power, water, and land” and that energy supply is “constrained globally.”

Anchors — GOOGL 1A “manufacturing and supply chain risks”; MSFT “datacenters depend on… permitted and buildable land, predictable energy”; NVDA (customer-side) “clean water and predictable energy”; META “components, power or network capacity”; AMZN energy contracts ≈200M MWh, ≈16-yr weighted-average duration.
2

Chip and component concentration

Specialized AI chips come from “a small number of qualified suppliers” (Alphabet); Amazon relies on “a limited group of suppliers for semiconductor products, including… graphics processing units.” NVIDIA names its own chokepoints.

Anchors — NVDA: TSMC and Samsung foundries, CoWoS packaging, SK Hynix/Micron/Samsung memory, Asia-Pacific concentration, lead times “extended beyond twelve months”; MSFT: “few qualified suppliers for certain components of our servers.”
3

Return on capital and excess capacity

The classic build-out risk, in the companies’ own words: investments “may not be commercially viable or may not result in an adequate return of capital,” and in a downturn “excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy.”

Anchors — GOOGL 1A; AMZN: may not “recoup our investments,” “excess or insufficient… data center capacity… and impairment charges”; META: AI initiatives “may not be successful,” impairment/abandonment charges named; MSFT: “may not achieve expected returns.”
4

The financing stack

The marginal data center is increasingly debt- and lease-funded. Tighter lease or credit markets raise the cost of the 2026–2031 commencement schedule directly.

Anchors — META $30.0B notes, $103.8B leases not yet commenced, ~$28B RVG threshold; GOOGL 2025 debt raise, $16.9B + $5.7B backstops; MSFT finance leases $27.1B→$46.2B; AMZN total contractual commitments $439.7B.
5

Depreciation-policy divergence

Reported margins are partly a policy choice, made in opposite directions the same year — a comparability trap located precisely where the capex is largest.

Anchors — AMZN shortened lives 6→5 yrs: −$1.0B net income (primarily AWS); META extended to 5.5 yrs: +$2.59B net income, +$1.00 per diluted share.
6

Ecosystem loops

Demand and supply in this cycle are partly the same counterparties. NVIDIA is a customer of the clouds it sells to; capital flows from compute providers into model developers; Microsoft holds capacity rights over its closest AI partner.

Anchors — NVDA $10.9B multi-year cloud-service agreements (R&D, DGX Cloud) and “our customers’ ability to invest in AI infrastructure” as a demand factor; AMZN $2.7B into Anthropic convertible notes (2025); MSFT–OpenAI: reciprocal revenue-sharing, right of first refusal on OpenAI’s new capacity needs.
Note

Every one of these risks is named by the companies themselves. The underwriting work is to price them, not to discover them.

08 · ConclusionsWhere this points in the value chain

Our read, drawn from the disclosed record only — where the six companies’ own commitments and constraints suggest the firm should look next.

1

Energy and power infrastructure

Four of six name power, land or water as the binding constraint on scaling. Alphabet is vertically acquiring the capability — the $4.8B Intersect acquisition (data-center and energy infrastructure) — and contracting two decades of supply (the $9.9B PPA, 2027–2047). Amazon already runs an energy book of ≈200M MWh with a ≈16-year weighted-average duration.

Watch — generation, transmission and long-tenor PPA counterparties: they sit upstream of every GPU deployed across the 2026–2031 commencement windows.
2

Financing and structured capital for data centers

$358.9B of leases not yet commenced; finance leases growing 70% a year at Microsoft; JV structures with residual-value guarantees (Meta’s Louisiana Venture: ~$27B development costs, ~$28B RVG threshold, $12.31B of leases from 2029); credit backstops at Alphabet; straight debt at Meta ($30.0B) and Alphabet (2025).

Watch — lessors, private credit and guarantee providers: whoever warehouses these claims is financing the cycle and holding its residual-value risk.
3

The compute supply chain

Committed orders are large and concentrated: NVIDIA’s $30.8B of inventory and supply obligations, Amazon’s $27.0B of PP&E already in accounts payable, Alphabet’s $113.0B of short-term purchase commitments (mostly technical infrastructure and inventory). NVIDIA’s own 10-K names the chokepoints: TSMC/Samsung foundry, CoWoS packaging, SK Hynix/Micron/Samsung memory.

Watch — NVIDIA is the only one of the six whose capex is trivial ($3.2B): this cycle monetizes through its P&L, not its balance sheet. The exposure runs through its suppliers and its customers’ commitments.
4

Refresh economics and the margin cycle

The cost of the 2024–26 build arrives in the income statement through 2027–29: D&A steps of +31% (Amazon), +45% (Microsoft) and +38% (Alphabet). If AI hardware ages on a five-year clock — Amazon’s stated rationale for shortening lives — effective refresh demand compounds and reported margins diverge from cash economics.

Watch — the useful-life notes in every FY2026 10-K. A second shortening would mark the refresh cycle as structural, not incidental.
The abstainer

Apple: $98.8B of FCF, 11% intensity, and no AI-infrastructure narrative anywhere in its FY2025 10-K. Its silence is a datum — the one deep pocket not yet bidding for power and chips. A change in that posture would widen the cycle’s demand base; until then, the wave is five companies deep.
