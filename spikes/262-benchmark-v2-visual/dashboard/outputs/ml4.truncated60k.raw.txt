    <ol class="take">
      <li><b>Who is spending.</b> Amazon is the anchor of the wave ($131.8B), followed by Alphabet ($91.4B), Meta ($69.7B cash / $72.2B incl. finance-lease principal) and Microsoft ($64.6B). NVIDIA ($3.2B) and Apple ($12.7B) are structurally capital-light — the wave is a hyperscaler story plus one silicon supplier.</li>
      <li><b>How sustainably.</b> Capex/OCF spans 94% (Amazon) to 5% (NVIDIA); FCF ranking inverts almost perfectly (Apple $98.8B &gt; Alphabet $73.3B &gt; Microsoft $71.6B &gt; NVIDIA $60.9B &gt; Meta $46.1B &gt; Amazon $7.7B). Amazon funds the build almost entirely from operating cash flow, leaving a thin $7.7B FCF buffer. (§03)</li>
      <li><b>Off the capex line.</b> Leases not yet commenced total $358.9B across the five filers that disclose the line (Amazon $96.4B, Meta $103.8B, Microsoft $92.7B, Alphabet $58.5B, NVIDIA $7.6B); purchase/contractual obligations add $84.8B–$149.1B per filer. Definitions differ by filer — read §04 before comparing. (§04)</li>
      <li><b>Guidance discipline.</b> Initial FY2025 guides in the pack: Amazon ~$100B, Alphabet ~$75B, Meta $60–65B (incl. finance-lease principal). Actuals landed above the initial guide in all three, presented side by side (the source computes no variance; Meta is not like-for-like vs the XBRL cash line). 2026 indications step up again where quantified — Meta ~$115–135B. (§05)</li>
      <li><b>Signals &amp; where to look next.</b> Accelerating: 2026 capex language, Amazon's server-life cut to 5 years, Blackwell ramp, long-dated energy deals. Constraining: power/water/land (Alphabet), GPU supplier concentration (Amazon, Microsoft), &gt;12-month lead times (NVIDIA). Apple's 10-K discloses no AI-infrastructure strategy at all. Look next at power &amp; energy, silicon &amp; memory supply, colocation/structured vehicles, builder financing, and depreciation regimes. (§06–§07)</li>

  <section id="next">
    <div class="sec-head"><span class="sec-num">07</span><div>
      <h2>Where to look next in the value chain — conclusions</h2>
      <p class="sec-sub">Ranked by evidence density in the two inputs. Each call lists the figures behind it and what would confirm or break it.</p>
    </div></div>

    <div class="play"><div class="n">1</div><div>
      <h4>Power &amp; energy infrastructure <span class="pr hi">HIGH PRIORITY</span></h4>
      <p>The gating constraint named in the filings is power (plus water and land), and the builders are locking it in for decades — often off the capex line.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Evidence:</b> Amazon ~200M MWh of energy contracts, ~16-year weighted-average remaining life, majority beyond 9 years (some contracts 20 years) · Alphabet $9.9B PPA (2027–2047; else ~$3.5B one-time + ownership of generation assets) and the $4.8B Intersect acquisition (data-center + energy infrastructure, close expected H1 2026) · Alphabet risk factor: scaling "increasingly constrained by the availability of power, water, and land" · Microsoft: datacenters depend on "predictable energy" · NVIDIA: power/water shortages could impair customers' data-center expansion.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Watch:</b> take-or-pay energy obligations inside purchase commitments (Alphabet's $149.1B includes energy take-or-pay); PPA/lease structures moving power capex off-balance-sheet; permitting timelines.</p>
    </div></div>

    <div class="play"><div class="n">2</div><div>
      <h4>Silicon, memory &amp; the component supply chain <span class="pr hi">HIGH PRIORITY</span></h4>
      <p>GPU and memory supply is the second gate — concentrated, lead-time-bound, and already being pre-paid.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Evidence:</b> Amazon and Microsoft flag "a limited group of suppliers" / "few qualified suppliers" for GPUs and server components · Alphabet: AI-chip supply "limited to a small number of qualified suppliers" · NVIDIA: lead times "more than 12 months", with premiums, deposits and long-term capacity agreements; $30.8B inventory/supply-capacity obligations (Blackwell-led) · named supply chain: TSMC and Samsung (foundry), SK Hynix / Micron / Samsung (memory), CoWoS packaging, Foxconn / Wistron / Fabrinet (assembly) · Amazon separately flags memory-chip volatility · Apple: single/limited-source components and yield-maturity constraints on new custom chips.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Watch:</b> foundry and CoWoS capacity, memory (HBM-adjacent) availability, GPU allocation across the four hyperscalers, and any shift from "constraints" to actual delivery shortfalls.</p>
    </div></div>

    <div class="play"><div class="n">3</div><div>
      <h4>Colocation, third-party data centers &amp; structured vehicles <span class="pr hi">HIGH PRIORITY</span></h4>
      <p>A large and growing share of the build is leased rather than owned — signed, not yet commenced, and partly underwritten by the hyperscalers themselves.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Evidence:</b> leases not yet commenced: Meta ~$103.77B (mostly data centers/colo, 2026–2030, terms to 30 years), Microsoft $92.7B (datacenters, FY2026–FY2031), Amazon $96.4B, Alphabet $58.5B (2026–2031) · Alphabet "entering into significant leasing arrangements with third party operators" plus backstops of $5.7B guarantees and $16.9B credit derivatives on third-party data-center leases (up to 15 years) · Meta's Louisiana Venture: 20% equity-method JV, ~$27B total estimated development costs, $12.31B lease from 2029, ~$28B residual-value-guarantee threshold (no liability recorded) · Alphabet's downturn warning: "excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy".</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Watch:</b> conversion of not-yet-commenced leases into obligations; credit-support and RVG triggers if utilization disappoints; colo pricing and availability.</p>
    </div></div>

    <div class="play"><div class="n">4</div><div>
      <h4>Financing &amp; balance-sheet capacity of the builders <span class="pr md">MEDIUM</span></h4>
      <p>The wave is still cash-flow funded at most, but the mix is shifting toward debt and leases — most visibly where FCF is thinnest.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Evidence:</b> Meta issued $30.0B of fixed-rate senior notes in Nov 2025 (net proceeds $29.91B) against $72.22B of capex · Alphabet raised debt in 2025 and "expect[s] to continue to assess the use of debt… and finance leases, primarily for data centers" · Microsoft's finance-lease liabilities jumped to $46.2B from $27.1B · Amazon: total Note 7 commitments $439.7B against $7.7B of FY2025 FCF at 94% intensity.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Watch:</b> whether Amazon's thin FCF forces external funding of the 2026 build; cost and covenants of lease-heavy structures; Alphabet's counterparty credit support.</p>
    </div></div>

    <div class="play"><div class="n">5</div><div>
      <h4>Depreciation regimes &amp; earnings quality across the six <span class="pr md">MEDIUM</span></h4>
      <p>Accounting choices are diverging in opposite directions — flattering some margins and burdening others — and will drive reported-margin comparability.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Evidence:</b> Amazon cut a subset of server/networking lives 6→5 years effective Jan 2025 (+$1.4B D&amp;A, −$1.0B net income) after a 5→6 extension in Jan 2024 · Meta extended most servers/network assets to 5.5 years (−$2.92B D&amp;A, +$2.59B net income, +$1.00/diluted share) · Alphabet 6 years on servers, depreciation $21.1B (from $15.3B) · Microsoft 2–6 years on computer equipment, depreciation $22.0B (from $15.2B) · NVIDIA 2–7 years · Apple: straight-line, no server lives disclosed.</p>
      <p class="ev"><b>Watch:</b> Amazon's D&amp;A step-up as its $534.1B gross PP&amp;E base rolls through 5-year lives (AWS D&amp;A already $21.45B in FY2025); margin comparisons across the six are not like-for-like.</p>
    </div></div>

    <div class="reading">
      <div><b>Takeaway.</b> The capex line understates the cycle; the committed cycle lives in §04's off-balance buckets. Of the five areas above, power/energy, silicon/memory supply, and colocation/structured vehicles carry the densest evidence in these two inputs — and they are where a slowdown would first appear (constraints) or where pricing power accrues (supply).</div>

        <li><b>Meta basis caveat.</b> Meta's initial guidance ($60–65B) includes finance-lease principal; the XBRL cash actual ($69.7B) excludes it. The variance is not like-for-like and is not calculated [S2].</li>
        <li><b>Off-balance buckets are not additive.</b> Leases-not-yet-commenced and purchase-obligation figures are as-disclosed categories that differ by filer (NVIDIA's include product components; Apple's lease line is not disclosed separately). They are shown side by side, never summed into a "total commitment".</li>
        <li><b>Amazon FCF thinness.</b> At 94% capex/OCF, FY2025 FCF of $7.7B leaves almost no buffer; a modest OCF disappointment or working-capital swing transmits ~1:1 to FCF, and the 2026 build is guided up.</li>
        <li><b>Demand circularity.</b> NVIDIA's risk factors list "our customers' ability to invest in AI infrastructure" as a demand factor, alongside non-cancellable purchase orders placed &gt;12 months ahead — a hyperscaler pause would transmit quickly to the supplier.</li>
        <li><b>Constraint risk cuts both ways.</b> Power, water, land, GPU supply and lead times could slow the build (Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, NVIDIA all flag them) — a risk to the builders, but pricing power for suppliers in §07 areas 1–2.</li>
        <li><b>Source limits.</b> Every figure comes from the two provided inputs only; sums and multiples computed from them (e.g., $373.4B total, ≈3.7×, $358.9B lease sum, 35% share) are labeled as calculations. No external data, no estimates; where a figure is absent this dashboard says "not disclosed".</li>
