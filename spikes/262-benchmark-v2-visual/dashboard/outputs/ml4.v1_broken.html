Sustainability is uneven. Capex/OCF: Amazon 94% > Meta 60% > Alphabet 56% > Microsoft 47% > Apple 11% > NVIDIA 5%. FCF inverts: Apple $98.8B, Alphabet $73.3B, Microsoft $71.6B, NVIDIA $60.9B, Meta $46.1B, Amazon $7.7B [S6][S7].

Reading. This dashboard answers “who is spending, how sustainably, and under which definition.” The strategic point: the reported capex line understates the committed build in Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft because leases not yet commenced, purchase obligations, construction commitments and energy/guarantee arrangements sit outside it. Why it matters: FCF today can look acceptable while future cash obligations are already contracted. Watch: not-yet-commenced leases rolling into capex/ROU, energy PPAs, GPU/server supply, and depreciation as AI assets age faster.

9 · Where the firm should look next — conclusions, risks, watch items

Conclusions for the value chain

  • Power / energy is the clearest choke point. Amazon ~200M MWh energy contracts, ~16-year weighted remaining life; Alphabet names power/water/land constraints and signed a Jan 2026 PPA with $9.9B of expected lease payments over 2027–2047 plus the $4.8B Intersect acquisition; Meta has 3–25 year clean-energy agreements and data-center power needs; Microsoft depends on “predictable energy”; NVIDIA flags customer power/water/land constraints. Look at PPAs, power developers, grid/interconnect and data-center energy solutions.
  • Data-center real estate, third-party operators and lease structures are where commitment sits outside capex. Not-yet-commenced leases: Meta $103.77B, Amazon $96.373B, Microsoft $92.7B, Alphabet $58.5B computed, NVIDIA $7.6B. Alphabet has credit derivatives notional $16.9B and guarantees $5.7B tied to third-party data-center leases; Meta’s Louisiana Venture has ~$27B committed development cost, ~$12.31B initial lease commitment and ~$28B RVG threshold. Look at colo operators, lease guarantors, construction managers and data-center landlords.
  • Servers, network and AI accelerators / memory are the hardware line. Alphabet: ~60% of technical infrastructure assets are servers/network equipment; Meta servers/network assets $98.040B plus CIP $50.521B; Amazon names GPU supplier constraints and shortened server life to five years; Microsoft names few qualified suppliers for servers/GPUs; NVIDIA discloses TSMC/Samsung, CoWoS, SK hynix/Micron/Samsung and Hon Hai/Wistron/Fabrinet; Apple flags single/limited-source custom components. Look at GPU/HBM supply, foundry/packaging, server OEMs, memory, and networking.
  • Depreciation / useful lives are a margin tell. Amazon cut a subset of server/network lives 6→5 years (+$1.4B D&A, −$1.0B net income); Meta extended most servers/network assets to 5.5 years (−$2.92B depreciation, +$2.59B net income); Alphabet PP&E depreciation $21.1B with six-year server/network life; Microsoft depreciation $22.0B; Apple PP&E depreciation $8.0B but no server/network useful lives disclosed; NVIDIA 2–7 years. Watch changes in estimates as AI assets age.
  • Cash-flow quality differs by design. Apple and NVIDIA convert nearly all OCF to FCF; Amazon retains only $7.7B. If the cycle turns, the first stress point is Amazon-style FCF compression, then Microsoft/Meta margin pressure, then supplier/order mismatch at NVIDIA.

Named risks

  • Definition risk: Meta guidance incl. finance-lease principal vs XBRL cash capex; Amazon pack capex vs cash capex vs unpaid PP&E; NVIDIA MD&A capex vs cash-flow line. Do not compute cross-company variances on mismatched bases [S2][S6].
  • Off-balance-sheet risk: leases not yet commenced, take-or-pay energy, purchase commitments, construction commitments, guarantees/credit derivatives and RVGs can become future cash capex or liabilities.
  • Supply / energy risk: power, water, land, permitting, GPUs, memory, single-source components and Asia-concentrated manufacturing are repeatedly named.
  • Demand / obsolescence risk: NVIDIA depends on customers’ AI infrastructure investment and data-center capacity/energy and carries non-cancellable purchase orders; Amazon warns fixed costs and data-center capacity misprediction; Meta flags impairment/abandonment of infrastructure projects; useful-life changes can move margins.
  • Disclosure gaps: initial FY2025 guidance not disclosed for Microsoft/NVIDIA/Apple; NVIDIA FY2020–FY2021 capex not disclosed; Apple has no disclosed AI/data-center strategy, capex trajectory, server useful lives, data-center-specific lease amounts or AI-infrastructure risk factor in the extract. Report as “not disclosed,” not estimate.
  • Comparability risk: fiscal years end in Dec (AMZN/GOOG/META), Jun 30 (MSFT), late Jan (NVDA) and Sep 27 (AAPL); compare fiscal years, not calendar quarters.

What to watch next

  • 2026 capex actuals vs the separate 2026 indications: Meta $115–135B; Alphabet “significantly increase”; Amazon increase; NVIDIA increase; Microsoft continued; Apple not disclosed.
  • Lease commencement schedules and ROU additions; purchase obligations becoming PP&E; accounts-payable capex at Amazon ($27.0B) and Microsoft ($6.9B).
  • Energy: PPAs, take-or-pay contracts, derivative-settled energy contracts, and customer power constraints at NVIDIA.
  • Useful-life and depreciation changes; server/network share of PP&E; impairment language.
  • Supplier concentration and capacity commitments: GPUs/HBM/memory, foundry/CoWoS, server/network OEMs, colo and power.
Reading. The capex wave is real, still guided up for 2026 where disclosed, and increasingly constrained by power, land and components rather than by demand alone. The firm’s next work should concentrate where cash is committed but not yet in capex: energy/grid, data-center leases/colo, and the AI silicon supply chain. Why it matters: these are the places where a slowdown would first show up as excess capacity, cancellation penalties, impairments or margin compression — and where an acceleration would tighten supply and pricing. Watch: the gap between reported capex/FCF and disclosed commitments; don’t let a low capex/OCF ratio at NVIDIA or Apple be mistaken for absence of exposure.
